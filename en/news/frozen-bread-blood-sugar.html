Freeze your bread, then toast it: does it really lower blood sugar? What the studies found

Share this article:

A trick that spread fast: put your bread in the freezer before you eat it

A slice of fresh white bread raises blood sugar quickly after eating — anyone who checks their glucose after breakfast knows this. But what if you changed how the bread itself is prepared, without changing the bread? In recent days a simple idea has spread widely: freeze the bread, thaw it, then toast it, and you will get a smaller blood-sugar rise. The idea is not entirely new; it goes back to a small study published in 2008, which returned to the spotlight after a report by Al Arabiya on 19 November 2026, and was then backed by a newer trial from 2023.

So what did the two studies actually measure? Is the difference big enough to matter? And does it mean white bread has become a healthy food? The short answer: the effect is real, but modest, the studies are small, and white bread stays white bread.

What exactly did the 2008 study do?

In a study published in the European Journal of Clinical Nutrition in 2008, researchers Burton and Lightowler asked 10 healthy adults, aged 22 to 59, to eat white bread in four different forms: fresh, toasted, frozen then defrosted, and frozen then defrosted then toasted. After each meal they measured the incremental area under the blood-glucose curve — the total rise in blood sugar over the following two hours, a more accurate measure than a single peak reading.

The result was clear in direction: for homemade bread, the curve area was 259 units with fresh bread, falling to 179 with freezing and defrosting, to 193 with toasting alone, and to 157 with freezing then toasting. For commercial bread, it fell from 253 fresh to 183 toasted, and to 187 frozen then toasted. In other words, the best combination — freeze then toast — cut the curve area by about 40% for homemade bread, and by about a quarter for commercial bread.

But keep the number in proportion: the study had only 10 participants, all of them healthy, the bread was white, and the measurement followed a single meal. It proves that preparation changes the glucose curve; it does not prove that this trick improves health over the long term.

Why does freezing change bread? Resistant starch

The explanation lies in what happens to starch when it cools. When bread is baked and then cooled or frozen, the starch molecules pack together again in a process called retrogradation, forming a type of starch known as resistant starch type 3 (RS3). This starch resists digestion in the small intestine and behaves like fibre: it is digested more slowly, so blood sugar rises less sharply, and part of it reaches the colon, where it feeds beneficial bacteria.

Toasting after freezing adds a second benefit: it dries the surface of the bread and makes it slightly firmer, slowing chewing and digestion further. That is why the combination of freezing and toasting beat either one alone in the study.

The 2023 trial: was the result confirmed 15 years later?

In 2023, researchers Yahya and Hashim ran a randomised trial on 32 healthy adults aged 18 to 50, comparing fresh bread with bread reheated after freezing. Peak blood glucose after eating was 132 mg/dl with fresh bread, versus 120 mg/dl with the reheated bread — a difference of about 12 points at the peak, modest but consistent with the 2008 study.

The trial added an important practical detail: freezing the bread for 3, 5 or 7 days gave similar results, so longer freezing did not add more resistant starch. You do not need to freeze bread for a whole week; ordinary freezing is enough.

The Al Arabiya report added another detail: in partially-baked fibre bread, freezing cut the glycaemic index by about 34%. That is a reminder that the type of bread itself remains the biggest factor: fibre bread already starts from a better place than white bread.

The limits of the trick: what it does not do

First, freezing does not remove the carbohydrates from bread; it only slows their digestion, and it does not turn white bread into a healthy food. Wholemeal bread, rye bread and sourdough remain better choices, because they contain fibre and nutrients that white bread lacks, however much you freeze it.

Second, both studies are small — 10 people and 32 people — and both were done in healthy people, not people with diabetes. People with diabetes must not change their treatment or doses based on this trick; it improves the glucose curve after a meal, it does not treat diabetes.

Third, the effect is modest: about 40% lower curve area in the best case for homemade bread, about a quarter for commercial bread, and about 12 points lower at the glucose peak in the 2023 trial. That is an improvement worth noting, but not a revolution.

Finally, there are stronger ways to blunt the blood-sugar rise after bread: eating bread with protein or healthy fat — such as eggs or nuts — also slows digestion, and walking after a meal helps muscles absorb glucose. The trick alone is no substitute for a balanced meal.

Tip

If you eat white bread every day, try this simple trick: slice the bread and freeze it, then thaw and toast it before eating, and eat it with eggs, cheese or nuts instead of jam alone. But do not treat it as a licence to eat more white bread — the stronger step is to move gradually to wholemeal, rye or sourdough bread, and to walk for ten minutes after a meal. And if you have diabetes, do not change your treatment or doses based on this trick; discuss any change to your diet with your doctor first.

The bottom line: a real improvement, but a modest one

Freezing bread then toasting it is a free and easy trick, backed by an understandable scientific mechanism — resistant starch — and by two consistent studies 15 years apart. It lowers the blood-sugar rise after eating by about a quarter to about 40% in curve area, and by about 12 points at the peak. But it does not turn white bread into a healthy food, it does not replace choosing better bread, a balanced meal and a walk after eating, or prescribed treatment for those who need it. Use the trick if you like it, but put it in its right place: a small improvement inside bigger habits.

  • Homemade bread, blood-glucose curve area: fresh 259 → frozen and defrosted 179 → toasted 193 → frozen, defrosted and toasted 157 mmol·min/l (about 40% lower in the best case).
  • Commercial bread, blood-glucose curve area: fresh 253 → toasted 183 → frozen, defrosted and toasted 187 mmol·min/l (about 25% lower).

  • Limits: two small studies (10 and 32 people) in healthy people; the 2008 study is old; the effect is modest; freezing does not remove the carbohydrates and does not turn white bread into a health food; people with diabetes must not change their treatment based on this.
