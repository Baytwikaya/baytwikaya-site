A gut bacterium helps burn visceral fat: a new discovery — but so far only in mice

Share this article:

Visceral fat: the body’s most dangerous fat

Not all fat is equal: visceral fat — the deep fat surrounding the organs inside the abdomen — is strongly linked to type 2 diabetes, heart disease and fatty liver, far more than the fat under the skin. Diet and movement help shrink it, but slowly, which is why scientists are looking for new ways to target it directly.

A new study published in Cell Host & Microbe in 2026, led by Tan and colleagues from Duke-NUS in Singapore and Duke University in the United States, found an unexpected candidate: a bacterium living in the gut that makes a special sugar which pushes the body to burn visceral fat.

What did the researchers discover?

The bacterium is called Clostridium immunis, and it makes a complex sugar — a phosphocholine-modified exopolysaccharide. This sugar lowers the level of an immune molecule called IL-22, which in turn raises the number of heat-producing cells carrying the protein UCP1 inside visceral fat, so energy burning increases specifically in that fat.

And strikingly, the effect was local: the increase in burning was concentrated in visceral fat, not in all body fat.

What happened to the mice?

In obese mice, a single oral dose of the bacterium cut visceral fat by about a third within one week — without the mice eating less or moving more. In other words, the effect did not come from less food or more activity, but from changing how the body itself handles energy.

The most important caveat: mice only

Here we must stop and pay attention: everything above happened in mice only, and there are no human data yet. The human gut, microbiome and immune system differ from those of mice, and we do not know the right dose for people or its long-term safety. The researchers have filed a patent and are seeking funding for human trials — the road to any treatment is still at its beginning, and there is no product to buy today. So beware any “gut bacteria” supplement claiming to burn belly fat on the basis of this news.

Tip

Visceral fat is reduced today by proven means: cutting sugary drinks and ultra-processed foods, eating more fibre and vegetables, moving regularly, and sleeping enough. Fermented foods and yogurt support gut health in general, but they are not this treatment — so do not buy an illusion in the name of science.

The bottom line: a clever mechanism, and a long wait

The discovery of Clostridium immunis and its sugar that stimulates visceral-fat burning is elegant science: a clear mechanism, and a strong, fast effect in mice — a third of visceral fat gone in a week with a single dose. But for now it is mouse science only, with no human data, and a patent seeking funding. So the news is a scientific promise, not a treatment — and the road from the mouse laboratory to the human clinic is long.
