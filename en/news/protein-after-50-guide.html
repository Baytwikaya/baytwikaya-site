Protein After Fifty: The Complete Guide

Share this article:

Protein after fifty: why does it become more important?

After the age of fifty, the body gradually begins to lose muscle mass, a natural process called age-related muscle loss, which speeds up if physical activity falls and protein in the diet falls. Muscles are not only for appearance; they are the basis of strength, balance and independence: the ability to climb stairs, carry shopping bags, rise from a chair, and prevent falls and fractures. Protein is also essential for bone health, for healing after illness or surgery, and for the immune system. The problem is that many older people eat less protein than they need, whether because of loss of appetite, difficulty chewing, living alone, or the mistaken belief that protein is only for athletes. In this guide we explain why your body needs more protein after fifty, what its best sources are in Moroccan cooking, how to spread it across the day, and when you should consult your doctor. The goal is to preserve your strength and independence for many years to come.

What is protein and what does it do in the body?

Protein is a basic component of every cell in the body; it builds muscles, bones, skin and hair, and enters into the formation of enzymes, hormones and antibodies that protect you from infection. Protein is made of small units called amino acids, some of which the body produces and some of which must come from food. Complete protein is the kind that contains all the essential amino acids, and it is found in animal sources such as eggs, fish, chicken and yoghurt, and in some plant sources such as soya, while other plant sources such as lentils and chickpeas need variety and combination with grains to give complete protein. The body needs protein every day, because it does not store it like fat, so it must be eaten at every meal. With advancing age, the body becomes less responsive to protein, meaning that the same meal builds less muscle than it built in youth, and for this reason older people need a slightly larger amount and a better distribution across the day, together with regular physical activity, to preserve their muscles.

How much protein do you need after fifty?

The right amount differs from one person to another according to weight, activity and health, and there is no single rule that suits everyone. General recommendations for healthy adults set a minimum for protein, but many nutrition experts consider that active older people need a higher amount than this minimum to preserve muscle, especially if they do resistance exercise. The simple practical rule is: make protein present at every meal, and do not put it all into one meal, because the body benefits from protein spread across the day more than from one large dose. For example, eggs in the morning, fish, chicken or lentils at lunch, and yoghurt, cheese or eggs at dinner is a better distribution than one large meat meal a day and the rest of the day bread and tea. If you have kidney disease, the situation is completely different: people with kidney disease may need to reduce protein, not increase it, so you must ask your doctor before increasing protein if you have a kidney or liver problem. Do not rely on supplements and powders without medical advice, because whole food remains the best choice for most people.

The best sources of protein in Moroccan cooking

Moroccan cooking is rich in varied and available sources of protein. Eggs are among the best and cheapest sources, containing complete protein that is easy to digest, and they can be eaten boiled or in an omelette with vegetables. Fish, especially sardines, gives excellent protein with omega-3, and is available at a reasonable price. Chicken and turkey are good choices if you remove the skin and cook them grilled or in a tagine. Yoghurt and soft cheese give protein with calcium, but watch the salt in some types of cheese. As for plant sources, lentils, chickpeas, broad beans and white beans are authentic Moroccan dishes rich in protein and fibre, cheap and available, and they can be combined with grains such as rice or couscous to give complete protein. Nuts such as almonds and walnuts give protein with beneficial fats, but in small amounts because they are rich in calories. In Ramadan, harira with lentils and chickpeas can be a good start for protein at iftar, followed by a main dish with fish, chicken or eggs.

Protein and strength exercise: an inseparable pair

Protein alone does not build muscle without movement, and movement alone is not enough without sufficient protein. Resistance exercise, exercise that makes the muscles work against resistance, such as lifting light weights, using resistance bands or your own body weight, is the strongest stimulus for preserving muscle after fifty. You do not need an expensive gym: you can start at home with simple exercises such as standing up and sitting down from a chair several times, climbing stairs, brisk walking, and carrying water bottles as light weights, then increase the difficulty gradually. Eating a meal with protein within a few hours after exercise helps the muscles benefit. Research suggests that combining sufficient protein with regular resistance exercise is the best strategy for preserving strength, balance and independence with advancing age. If you have joint pain or a heart problem, ask your doctor before starting a new exercise programme, and start slowly and under supervision if possible, especially in the first weeks.

Common difficulties and practical solutions

Many older people face difficulties in eating enough protein, and every difficulty has a practical solution. If you have lost your appetite, eat small frequent meals instead of three large ones, and make protein the first thing you eat in the meal before you feel full. If you have difficulty chewing, choose soft protein-rich foods such as boiled eggs, yoghurt, soft fish, mashed lentils and soft cheese, and avoid tough meat. If you live alone, cook a quantity to last two days and keep it in the fridge, or use canned sardines, eggs and yoghurt, which need no long cooking. If your budget is limited, eggs, lentils, chickpeas, broad beans and sardines are among the cheapest and best sources of protein, and you do not need red meat every day. If you are vegetarian or eat less meat, vary between legumes, grains, nuts, eggs and yoghurt so that you get all the amino acids. Remember that drinking enough water matters when protein increases, and constipation may increase if fibre falls, so take care to eat vegetables and fruit with protein.

A practical day rich in protein after fifty

Here is an example of a balanced day in Morocco: in the morning, boiled eggs or an omelette with vegetables, with whole bread, olive oil and a cup of unsweetened green tea. At lunch, a chicken or fish tagine with vegetables, or lentils with cumin and salad, and whole bread in moderation. In the afternoon, a cup of yoghurt with a handful of almonds or three dates. At dinner, vegetable soup with chickpeas, or eggs with salad, or soft cheese with whole bread. Between meals, drink water regularly, and move for half an hour a day, walking or at home. If you are ill or recovering from surgery, you may need more protein, so ask your doctor or dietitian about a plan that suits your condition. Do not forget sufficient sleep, because muscles recover and grow during sleep, and lack of sleep increases appetite for sugary foods and reduces energy for movement. With this simple pattern, you preserve your strength and health without feeling that you follow a strict regime.

Tip

Spread protein across the three meals of the day, and make every meal contain a clear protein source such as eggs, fish, lentils or yoghurt.

The bottom line: protein at every meal, and movement every day

After fifty, protein becomes more important, not less, because it preserves your muscles, your strength and your independence. Spread it across the day's meals, choose its varied sources in Moroccan cooking, from eggs and fish to lentils and chickpeas, and combine it with simple resistance exercise and daily walking. If you have a kidney or liver problem, or take medicines, consult your doctor before increasing protein or taking supplements. Your strength in the years to come starts on your plate today.
