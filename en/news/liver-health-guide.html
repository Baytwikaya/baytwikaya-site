Liver Health: The Complete Guide

Share this article:

What is the liver and why is it so important?

The liver is the largest internal organ in the body, located in the upper right part of the abdomen under the ribs. It works as a factory and a filter at the same time: it makes proteins and substances needed for blood clotting, stores energy as glycogen, helps digest fats by producing bile, processes medicines and chemicals, and cleans the blood of toxins. Remarkably, the liver can regenerate itself after partial damage, but this ability has limits, and if damage continues for years, scars build up and its function gradually weakens. Most liver diseases develop silently without clear symptoms at first, which is why prevention and early detection are very important. In this guide we explain how the liver works, its most important diseases, their causes, and how to protect your liver in daily life.

The main liver diseases

One of the most common liver diseases is fatty liver disease, which is the build-up of fat in liver cells. It may be linked to obesity, diabetes, high cholesterol, and lack of movement, or to alcohol. In many cases it is mild, but in some people it progresses to inflammation and then to fibrosis, the build-up of scar tissue that hinders liver function. Liver diseases also include viral hepatitis, especially hepatitis B and C, viral infections that can become chronic and cause gradual liver damage. There are other diseases such as cirrhosis resulting from various causes, liver cancer, which usually appears after years of cirrhosis, and less common inherited and immune diseases. Some medicines, supplements, and herbs can also harm the liver if taken in large doses or for a long time without medical supervision. The important message is that many liver diseases can be prevented or slowed if they are detected early and their cause is treated.

What symptoms should you pay attention to?

In the early stages, liver diseases usually cause no symptoms, which is why a doctor sometimes discovers them by chance through a routine blood test showing raised liver enzymes. As the disease advances, symptoms may appear, such as persistent tiredness, loss of appetite, nausea, pain or heaviness in the upper right abdomen, yellowing of the skin and the whites of the eyes, known as jaundice, swelling of the abdomen or legs, easy bruising, itchy skin, dark urine, and pale stools. These symptoms are not specific to the liver alone and can have other causes, but they call for seeing a doctor without delay, especially if jaundice, swelling, vomiting blood, or black stools appear, because these are serious signs that need urgent medical care. Do not try to interpret your test results yourself from the internet; show them to your doctor, who will decide whether further tests are needed, such as ultrasound or tests for viruses.

What are the risk factors?

The most important risk factors are alcohol, because drinking too much for years is one of the main causes of liver damage in the world. Others include excess weight and obesity, especially fat around the abdomen, type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol and triglycerides, and lack of physical activity. They also include infection with viral hepatitis B and C, which spread through blood and body fluids, and hepatitis B can be prevented by vaccination. They also include some medicines, such as taking too much paracetamol or some painkillers, and using untrusted herbs and supplements. They also include exposure to some chemicals at work without protection. In Morocco, prevention is becoming more important with the spread of obesity, diabetes, and lack of movement, and with some people continuing to use herbal mixtures without medical supervision, thinking they are safe because they are natural, when they may be harmful to the liver.

How can you protect your liver?

The best protection for the liver begins with lifestyle. Avoid alcohol completely, because there is no safe amount for the liver in people who have liver disease, and stopping drinking is one of the most important steps in treatment and prevention. Keep a healthy weight through balanced food and regular movement, because gradual weight loss may reduce fat built up in the liver. Eat food rich in vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes, and fish, and cut down on sugar, sweetened drinks, processed foods, and fried foods. Walk briskly on most days of the week. Make sure you are vaccinated against hepatitis B if you are not vaccinated, and ask your doctor about it, especially if you work in healthcare or have risk factors. Do not share injection equipment or personal shaving tools with others, and make sure tools are sterilised in beauty centres and tattoo studios. Do not take medicines, herbs, or supplements without consulting a doctor or pharmacist, read the labels, and follow the prescribed doses.

Tests and treatment

If the doctor suspects liver disease, they may request blood tests including liver enzymes, tests for viral hepatitis, an ultrasound scan, and sometimes other tests to assess the degree of fibrosis. Treatment depends on the cause: if the cause is alcohol, stopping drinking is essential. If the cause is fatty liver, the main treatment is losing weight, eating healthily, moving, and controlling diabetes and cholesterol. If the cause is a viral infection, there are effective medicines, especially for hepatitis C, which can cure the infection in many cases, and medicines for hepatitis B that control the virus. There is no single medicine that treats every liver disease, and there is no proven herb that cleans the liver or treats fibrosis, despite claims spread on the internet. Beware of liver cleanse and detox products; they are not treatment and may harm your liver. Keep your follow-up appointments with your doctor, because some liver diseases need regular monitoring for years.

Misconceptions about the liver

One misconception is that the liver is cleansed by juices and harsh fasting; the truth is that the liver cleans itself when it is healthy, it does not need a cleansing diet, and harsh fasting may harm it. Another is that natural herbs are always safe; the truth is that some herbs cause serious liver damage. Another is that fatty liver is a minor problem not worth attention; the truth is that it may progress to fibrosis in some people and must be followed up. Another is that liver disease affects only people who drink alcohol; the truth is that fatty liver linked to obesity and diabetes is very common even in people who never drink alcohol. Another is that yellowing of the eyes appears early; the truth is that it usually appears at advanced stages, which is why you should not wait for symptoms to get checked if you have a risk factor.

Tip

Protect your liver with simple steps: avoid alcohol completely, keep a healthy weight, eat a balanced diet, and do not take medicines or herbs without consulting a doctor or pharmacist.

The bottom line: your liver works for you, so work for it

The liver is a silent organ that works day and night to clean your blood, store your energy, and digest your food, and it deserves your daily care. Avoid alcohol, keep a healthy weight, eat a balanced diet, move regularly, make sure you are vaccinated, and do not take medicines or herbs without medical advice. If you have a risk factor or worrying symptoms appear, consult your doctor early, because early detection changes the course of the disease. A healthy liver means a stronger body and a longer, better life.
