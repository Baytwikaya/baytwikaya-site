Type 2 diabetes thins the brain’s cortex faster: controlling blood sugar protects the brain too

Share this article:

Diabetes harms not only the eyes and kidneys — the brain too

Type 2 diabetes is known to harm the eyes, kidneys, nerves and blood vessels, but its relationship with the brain itself has been less clear. Does diabetes speed up brain ageing even before any memory weakness appears?

A new study in the journal Brain, led by Tsiknia and colleagues and published in November 2026, tried to answer: 1,298 cognitively healthy adults, with a mean age of 65, underwent brain imaging and cognitive tests, and were followed for about 3 years.

What did the study find?

Type 2 diabetes was linked to faster thinning of the brain’s cortex — the outer layer responsible for thinking and memory — in 7 regions of the frontal, parietal and occipital lobes. And this link was independent of Alzheimer’s disease and of vascular burden, meaning it was not merely a reflection of them.

The researchers also found that a higher baseline HbA1c — that is, poorer blood-sugar control — was linked to faster thinning, and that the thinning explained about 13% of the faster decline in processing speed, the speed at which the brain handles information.

What does cortical thinning mean?

The brain’s cortex is a thin layer of nerve cells covering the brain, and it gradually thins with age in everyone. The study says diabetes speeds up this natural thinning. Thinning here is a marker of brain ageing, not dementia itself — the participants were cognitively healthy — but it is linked to slower processing speed, one of the first functions to decline with age.

Study limits

The study is observational: the link is not proof that diabetes is the direct cause, since accompanying factors may play a role. Follow-up was also only about 3 years, which is short for measuring slow brain changes, and the participants were cognitively healthy, so we do not yet know whether faster thinning actually turns into more dementia. Even so, the fact that the result was independent of Alzheimer’s and vascular disease makes it worth attention.

Tip

If you have type 2 diabetes, know that controlling your blood sugar protects your brain too, not only your eyes and kidneys: stick to your treatment and to the HbA1c targets agreed with your doctor, keep a healthy weight, move regularly, keep blood pressure controlled and sleep enough, and avoid smoking. And see your doctor regularly — preventing diabetes complications starts before symptoms appear.

The bottom line: high blood sugar weighs on the brain too

The Brain study of 1,298 cognitively healthy adults says type 2 diabetes speeds up thinning of the brain’s cortex in 7 regions, independently of Alzheimer’s and vascular disease, that poorer blood-sugar control increases the thinning, and that the thinning explains about 13% of the faster decline in processing speed. The study is observational and short, but its message is clear: controlling blood sugar today is an investment in tomorrow’s brain.
